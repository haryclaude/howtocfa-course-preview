CFA Level I: Time & Cost – My Honest Assessment

Understand the exact time and financial commitment for CFA Level I. Get Harmeet Hora's 6-month study plan and breakdown of costs.

Harmeet Hora IIT & IIM Alumni | CFA Charterholder
Mar 3, 2026

Question from a mentee: How much time and money required?

This is an excellent and fundamental question, one that every serious CFA candidate must consider upfront. As an IIT-IIM alumnus and CFA charterholder who has mentored hundreds of aspiring finance professionals, I can tell you that clarity on commitment — both in terms of time and finances — is paramount for success. It is not about raw intelligence; it is about structured effort and consistent discipline.

Let us get straight to my honest assessment.

The Time Commitment for CFA Level I

You are asking about a significant professional milestone, and it demands a serious, but achievable, time investment. My recommendation for CFA Level I preparation is a structured six-month period. This is not an arbitrary number; it is a carefully calibrated approach designed to ensure comprehensive understanding and robust revision.

The 6-Month Blueprint: 4+2 Strategy

My recommended strategy breaks down into two distinct phases:

  1. Four Months for Curriculum Mastery: This period is dedicated to systematically working through the entire CFA Level I curriculum. Your goal here is to build a strong foundational understanding of every topic area.
  2. Two Months for Mock Exams and Revision: This phase is equally critical. It is where you consolidate your knowledge, identify weaknesses, refine your exam strategy, and build endurance through rigorous practice.

Let us explore each phase in detail.

Phase 1: Curriculum Mastery (Months 1-4)

During these four months, your primary focus must be on understanding the concepts, not just memorizing them. The CFA Institute curriculum is extensive, covering ten distinct topic areas.

Your Weekly Commitment: You should aim for an average of 15-20 hours of dedicated study per week. This is a non-negotiable commitment. For many working professionals, this translates to:

  • 2-3 hours on weeknights.
  • 8-10 hours over the weekend.

This consistent effort ensures that the material stays fresh and builds incrementally. Skipping weeks will lead to significant backlogs and increased stress.

How to Approach the Curriculum:

  • Sequential Learning: Follow a logical sequence of topics. Start with foundational areas like Quantitative Methods and Economics, then move into Financial Statement Analysis, Corporate Issuers, and Equity Investments. Fixed Income, Derivatives, and Alternative Investments often require more conceptual heavy lifting. Ethics should be integrated throughout your study, not left until the end.
  • Active Engagement: Simply reading notes is not enough. You must actively engage with the material. Solve end-of-chapter questions diligently. Make your own summary notes. Discuss concepts with study partners if you have them.
  • Leverage Structured Resources: A well-designed lecture series can significantly streamline this phase. It provides context, simplifies complex topics, and structures your learning path. This is where the “power of concept and context” truly comes into play.

Phase 2: Revision and Mock Exams (Months 5-6)

This is where you transform your knowledge into exam-ready performance. Many candidates underestimate this phase, and that is a critical mistake.

The Role of Mock Exams: You should aim to take at least 4-6 full-length mock exams during these two months. These are not merely practice sessions; they are diagnostic tools.

  • Identify Weaknesses: Mocks will brutally expose areas where your understanding is incomplete or incorrect.
  • Time Management: They train you to pace yourself and complete the exam within the allotted time. The CFA exam is as much a test of endurance and time management as it is of knowledge.
  • Exam Simulation: They familiarize you with the exam format, question styles, and the pressure of a timed test environment.

Effective Revision Strategies:

  • Review Mock Results Thoroughly: Do not just look at your score. Analyze every incorrect answer. Understand why you made a mistake. Was it a conceptual gap, a calculation error, or a misinterpretation of the question?
  • Targeted Review: Focus your revision on your weakest areas identified by the mocks. Revisit the curriculum material for those specific topics.
  • Formula Recall: Create a comprehensive formula sheet and practice recalling formulas quickly and accurately.
  • Ethics Review: Ethics is a significant portion of the exam and can be a differentiator. Ensure you understand the Standards of Professional Conduct thoroughly.

Why 6 Months?

Some candidates attempt to prepare in shorter durations, and a few succeed. However, for the vast majority, this leads to superficial learning and increased stress. Six months allows for:

  • Depth of Understanding: You can truly internalize complex concepts, not just skim over them.
  • Ample Revision: Adequate time to review, practice, and build confidence.
  • Work-Life Balance: It is demanding, but manageable alongside a full-time job without leading to complete burnout.

Conversely, extending preparation beyond six months without a clear reason can lead to fatigue and forgetting earlier material. Six months strikes the right balance.

The Financial Investment for CFA Level I

The financial outlay for the CFA Level I examination involves two primary components: fees paid directly to the CFA Institute and costs for third-party study materials.

CFA Institute Fees

These are mandatory and vary based on when you register. It is always prudent to register early to save a substantial amount.

  1. One-Time Enrollment Fee: This is paid only once when you register for your first CFA exam (Level I).
    • Approximately USD 350 (around ₹29,000 to ₹30,000, subject to exchange rates).
  2. Exam Registration Fee: This is paid for each exam level you attempt.
    • Early Registration: Approximately USD 940 (around ₹78,000 to ₹80,000).
    • Standard Registration: Approximately USD 1,250 (around ₹104,000 to ₹106,000).

Total Official Fees: If you register early, your total official fees for Level I will be in the range of ₹1.07 lakhs to ₹1.10 lakhs. This is a significant investment, and it underscores the importance of taking your preparation seriously.

Note: These figures are approximate and subject to change by the CFA Institute. Always check the official CFA Institute website for the most current fee structure.

Third-Party Study Material Costs

While the CFA Institute provides its curriculum, most candidates find third-party study providers invaluable for structured learning, condensed notes, practice questions, and mock exams. The cost for these can vary widely, from a few thousand rupees to over a lakh.

My Recommendation for Value: You will find that our resources at HowToCFA are designed to be extremely cost-effective. We believe that high-quality, structured preparation should be accessible to all serious candidates. Our detailed curriculum, lecture series, and demo sessions are available at a very nominal price. This ensures you receive:

  • Concept-driven lectures: Simplifying complex topics.
  • Structured sequencing: Guiding you through the curriculum efficiently.
  • Practice questions and mocks: Essential for application and revision.

When evaluating third-party materials, consider the return on investment. A slightly higher upfront cost for a truly effective program can save you the much larger cost of re-taking the exam, not to mention the opportunity cost of lost time.

Hidden or Overlooked Costs

Do not forget to account for these smaller, but still relevant, expenses:

  • Financial Calculator: A specific CFA-approved calculator (e.g., Texas Instruments BA II Plus or HP 12C) is mandatory. This is a one-time purchase, typically ₹3,000-₹5,000.
  • Travel to Exam Center: Depending on your location, there may be travel and accommodation costs associated with reaching the exam center.
  • Opportunity Cost: This is perhaps the largest “hidden” cost. The hours you dedicate to studying are hours you cannot spend on other activities, whether professional or personal. This underscores the need for efficient and effective study.

My Personal Take: An Investment, Not an Expense

Looking at these figures, you might feel daunted. Here is my perspective: view this as a strategic investment in your long-term career. The CFA charter is a globally recognized credential that opens doors to advanced roles in investment management, research, portfolio management, and more. The initial outlay in time and money is a small price to pay for the significant career advantages it provides.

Success on the CFA exam is not about being the smartest person in the room. It is about being the most disciplined and consistent. Start early, stick to your plan, and leverage high-quality, structured resources. The “4 months for curriculum + 2 months for mocks and revision” strategy, coupled with a commitment to consistent effort, is your clearest path to success.
