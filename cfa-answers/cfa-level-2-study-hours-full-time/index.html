CFA Level 2 Study Hours: Working Professional Guide

Harmeet Hora, an IIT-IIM alumnus and CFA charterholder, details how many hours working professionals should study for CFA Level 2. Get practical strategies.

Harmeet Hora IIT & IIM Alumni | CFA Charterholder
Mar 2, 2026

Question from a mentee: How many hours should I study per day for CFA Level 2 while working full time?

As an IIT-IIM alumnus and CFA charterholder, I understand the challenge of balancing a demanding full-time career with the rigorous requirements of the CFA Program. I have mentored hundreds of candidates through this journey, and the question of study hours, particularly for Level 2, is one of the most frequent. It is a valid concern, as Level 2 is a significant step up in complexity from Level 1.

The quick summary you have already seen is a good starting point: aiming for 15-18 hours per week, consistently applied over six months, is my core recommendation. If you can manage 20-22 hours weekly, that will provide a comfortable buffer. Let us break down how to achieve this without compromising your professional commitments or personal well-being.

Understanding the CFA Level 2 Challenge

Many working professionals ask, “Can I truly clear the CFA exam?” My answer is always an unequivocal “yes.” It is not about innate brilliance; it is about discipline, a clear strategy, and consistent effort. However, Level 2 demands a different approach compared to Level 1.

Why Level 2 Demands More

CFA Level 2 is often considered the most challenging of the three exams. It transitions from the foundational knowledge of Level 1 to a deeper application of investment tools and concepts, primarily through item sets—mini-cases with multiple-choice questions.

  • Increased Complexity: The material itself is more intricate. Topics like Financial Reporting and Analysis (FRA), Fixed Income, Equity Investments, and Derivatives are covered in greater depth. You will move beyond definitions to complex calculations, valuations, and scenario analyses.
  • Application Focus: The exam questions require you to apply concepts to specific situations, not just recall facts. This means rote memorization, which might have helped in some parts of Level 1, is far less effective here.
  • Interconnectedness: Many Level 2 topics are highly interconnected. A strong grasp of FRA is crucial for Equity Valuation, and Quantitative Methods underpin much of Fixed Income and Derivatives.

The CFA Institute’s 300-Hour Guideline: A Starting Point

The CFA Institute suggests approximately 300 hours of study per level. In my experience, for Level 2, this is a minimum. Candidates with strong finance backgrounds might find 300-350 hours sufficient. For those transitioning from non-finance roles, or if you struggled with Level 1 concepts, planning for 350-400 hours provides a more realistic buffer. The key is that these hours must be effective hours, not just time spent with a book open.

Crafting Your Study Schedule as a Working Professional

Time is the CFA candidate’s scarcest resource. I have seen brilliant candidates fail because they ran out of time during the exam, and I have seen average students pass because they managed their time impeccably. For a deeper dive into overall time management strategies, I recommend reading our guide on CFA Exam Time Management.

The 6-Month Advantage

For working professionals, a six-month preparation window is ideal for Level 2. This allows you to accumulate the necessary 350-400 hours without resorting to unsustainable daily study loads. Here is what the weekly hours look like for different preparation periods:

Preparation PeriodWeekly Hours Needed (approx.)
6 months15-18 hours
5 months18-22 hours
4 months22-25 hours

Pushing into a 3 or 4-month window for Level 2 while working full-time is extremely challenging and significantly increases the risk of burnout. I always advise my mentees to prioritize a longer, more sustainable study period.

Weekly Hour Targets: The Sweet Spot

To hit 15-18 hours per week, you need a disciplined, consistent approach. This does not necessarily mean studying every single day, but it does mean frequent engagement with the material.

Let us consider a breakdown:

  • Weekdays: Aim for 1.5 to 2 hours per day, 4-5 days a week. This accumulates to 6-10 hours.
  • Weekends: Dedicate longer, focused blocks. Two sessions of 3-4 hours each on Saturday and Sunday can add 6-8 hours.
  • Total: This structured approach easily gets you to 12-18 hours, with flexibility.

Structuring Your Weekday Study

Consistency is paramount. Even if you are tired after work, a focused session is better than no session.

  • Morning Sessions (6:00 AM - 7:30 AM): This is often the most productive time. Your mind is fresh, and there are fewer distractions. Dedicate 90 minutes to new material or reviewing complex concepts.
  • Evening Sessions (9:00 PM - 10:30 PM): After dinner, dedicate another 90 minutes to practice questions, reviewing notes, or watching video lectures. This reinforces what you studied earlier or helps you tackle a new, smaller topic.
  • Lunch Break (30 minutes): Use this time for flashcards, quick review of formulas, or solving a few practice problems. This is a bonus and helps keep concepts fresh.

The key is to make these sessions non-negotiable appointments in your calendar. Treat them with the same importance as a client meeting.

Maximizing Your Weekends

Weekends are your opportunity for deep dives and catching up.

  • Longer Blocks: Schedule 3-4 hour blocks for tackling heavy topics like FRA or Fixed Income. These longer sessions allow you to get into a rhythm and cover substantial ground.
  • Mix Activities: Do not just read. Use weekends for mock exams (later in your preparation), detailed problem-solving, reviewing your weak areas, and consolidating notes.
  • Dedicated Revision: Sunday mornings can be excellent for reviewing the entire week’s material, solidifying understanding before the new week begins.

Strategic Topic Approach for Level 2

Unlike Level 1, where all topics are relatively new, Level 2 builds on that foundation significantly. Your study plan must reflect the weights and complexity of Level 2 topics.

Key Differentiators in Level 2 Topics

While the topics have similar names, their depth changes drastically:

  • Financial Reporting and Analysis (FRA): This is a monster in Level 2, often requiring a deep understanding of complex accounting standards, intercorporate investments, and multinational operations. It is critical for Equity Valuation.
  • Equity Investments: Moves from basic ratios to advanced valuation models (e.g., residual income, free cash flow models).
  • Fixed Income: Involves yield curve strategies, credit analysis, and complex bond valuation.
  • Derivatives: Expands significantly into option strategies, swaps, and credit derivatives.
  • Ethics: Remains crucial. While the principles are similar to Level 1, the scenarios are often more nuanced and complex, requiring careful application of the Code and Standards.

My Personal Recommendation

My recommendation is to approach CFA Level 2 with a clear strategy and unwavering discipline. Do not underestimate the depth and application required. Aim for the 15-18 hours per week minimum, spread over six months. Prioritize active learning, consistent practice, and rigorous revision. Your “why” must be strong enough to sustain you through the challenging periods. Remember, the CFA charter is a marathon, not a sprint. Your consistent effort will compound into success.
