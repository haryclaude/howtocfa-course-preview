CFA Topper Study Strategy: Daily Routine & Tips

Over the years, having navigated the finance landscape from IIT to IIM and earning my CFA charter, I have mentored hundreds of CFA candidates. Many of them have gone on to achieve outstanding results, including clearing their exams in the 90th percentile. Their journeys offer invaluable lessons.

This article distills the core strategies, daily routines, and mindset shifts that have consistently led my mentees to success. This is not about innate brilliance; it is about structured effort, discipline, and a clear understanding of the CFA program’s demands.

1. The CFA Curriculum: A Generalist’s Challenge

The CFA Program is designed to provide you with a comprehensive, generalist understanding of the finance world. It covers ten diverse topics, from quantitative methods to ethics, financial statement analysis, and portfolio management. This breadth is a significant advantage for your career, opening doors to roles in credit analysis, investment banking, hedge fund management, equity research, and more. For a deeper dive into potential roles, you can explore our guide on CFA career paths in India.

However, this generalist nature presents its own challenge. You must grasp a substantial amount of material in depth across all subjects. The curriculum is intentionally designed to test not only your knowledge but also your patience, perseverance, and, critically, your discipline. Without a disciplined approach, you will find it difficult to connect the dots across these ten distinct yet interconnected subjects.

Many candidates feel overwhelmed at the outset. This is normal. The key is to acknowledge the vastness and then break it down into manageable parts.

2. Crafting Your Study Plan: Flexibility is Key

One of the most common questions I receive is about the “best” sequence for studying the CFA topics. My honest assessment, based on the experiences of numerous successful mentees, is that there is no single, universally superior sequence. Your study plan must be adaptable to your personal learning style and background.

Anjali, one of my mentees who cleared Level 1 with a strong score, found that a rigid sequence did not work for her. She started by exploring Kaplan notes for a few weeks to gauge her initial understanding, then formally registered. With an MBA in Finance, she had some foundational knowledge.

Here are the flexible approaches you can consider for topic sequencing:

  • Start with Familiar Topics: This builds confidence and momentum. Anjali began with Corporate Issues because its language and concepts were familiar from her prior studies. This allowed her to gain an “immediate win.”
  • Tackle Difficult Topics First: Some prefer to get the most challenging subjects out of the way early. If you have a subject you fear, completing it first can lift a significant burden from your shoulders.
  • Prioritize by Weightage: You might decide to focus on topics with higher exam weightage first, ensuring you dedicate ample time to them.
  • Mix Subjects: To avoid boredom and maintain engagement, mix a challenging topic with an easier or more familiar one. Studying one subject for too long can lead to fatigue.

What matters is that you keep adjusting your strategy as you progress. There is no hardcore rule you must follow to the letter. Your journey is unique. The ultimate goal is to complete the entire syllabus effectively before the exam date, regardless of the precise order in which you studied the topics.

For a detailed breakdown of what each topic entails, you may refer to our CFA Level 1 topics comprehensive guide.

3. The Power of Consistent Effort: Daily Routines

The CFA exam is not about sporadic bursts of intense study; it is about consistent, disciplined effort over an extended period. Many candidates fixate on the number of hours they must study each day. My mentees have shown me that quality and consistency often trump rigid hourly targets.

Anjali, for instance, did not track her study in terms of hours. Instead, she focused on completing a defined portion—perhaps half a chapter or an entire small chapter, along with its practice questions. If it took her two hours, she was done. If it took six, she put in the six hours. This approach prevented unnecessary stress and distraction from clock-watching. However, if you are someone who thrives on a fixed schedule, then by all means, set specific hours. The point is to find what works for you to ensure the material is covered.

Vedant, another high-scoring mentee, adopted a more structured approach to his daily routine. He started by dedicating 3-4 hours per day during the initial six months of his preparation. As the exam drew closer, particularly in the last six months leading up to his revision period, he ramped up his study time significantly, investing 9-10 hours daily. He even compensated for less study time on weekends due to travel with longer weekday sessions.

This gradual increase in study intensity is a common and effective strategy. It allows you to build stamina and mental capacity over time.

The “Average” Student’s Success

Many aspiring candidates ask if the CFA is only for academic toppers. Vedant’s experience provides a powerful answer. He scored 75% in his 12th-grade exams and even struggled with mathematics, scoring 45%. Yet, he achieved an impressive 79-80 percentile in CFA Level 1. This demonstrates that your prior academic record does not define your potential for CFA success.

What Vedant possessed was a clear “why”—the exam was an emotion, a significant part of his life’s ambition. When you are genuinely interested and passionate about what you are learning, you will naturally flourish. The CFA program is logical, not about rote memorization of complex mathematical formulas. It is about understanding concepts and their practical application.

My rule of thumb for Level 1 preparation is 90 minutes of dedicated study every day for six months. This consistency is far more effective than cramming. If 90 minutes is not feasible due to work or other commitments—a common scenario for CFA for working professionals—then aim for at least 45-60 minutes daily. The key is to maintain momentum. Topics are interconnected; for example, Time Value of Money from Quantitative Methods is crucial for valuation in Fixed Income and Equity Investments. Losing momentum means you might forget earlier concepts, making it harder to link them.

4. Mastering Key Topics and Tools

Success in the CFA exam is less about memorizing facts and more about deep conceptual understanding.

Shun Rote Learning

Indian academic systems often emphasize rote learning. The CFA Program is fundamentally different. It is highly logical and conceptual. If you rely on memorization, you will flounder when faced with multiple-choice questions that test your understanding from different angles. You must understand why a concept works, not just what it is. This logical approach is what allows candidates, even those with average math backgrounds, to excel.

Every Topic Matters

Another common pitfall is attempting to “average out” your score by excelling in some topics and neglecting others. The CFA Institute emphasizes a well-rounded understanding of finance. To achieve a high percentile, you must aim to score at least 60% in every single topic area. You cannot compensate for a very low score in one section with exceptionally high scores in others. This means a balanced effort across all ten subjects.

Focus on Challenging Topics

Certain topics tend to be more challenging, especially for candidates without a strong finance or accounting background. These include:

  • Financial Statement Analysis (FSA): This is a vast subject. If you do not have an accounting background, you might initially find it uncomfortable. Dedicate significant time to CFA Level 1 FRA to build a solid foundation.
  • Quantitative Methods: For non-engineering or non-math backgrounds, statistics and hypothesis testing can be particularly tough. Focus on the logical underpinnings and practical applications, rather than getting bogged down by complex formulas. You can learn more about this topic in our CFA Level 1 Quantitative Methods guide.
  • Ethics: This topic carries a significant weightage and is highly subjective. It requires extensive practice to understand the nuances of ethical decision-making in financial contexts. You must practice scenario-based questions to grasp why one option is correct over others. Refer to our CFA Level 1 Ethics article for more insights.

I have crafted my CFA Level 1 lecture series with practical examples and real-world industry applications, including Excel integration, specifically to help you master these complex areas and become not just exam-ready, but also job-ready. Developing essential skills for financial jobs is crucial.

Master Your Financial Calculator

The CFA exam requires a specific financial calculator. You cannot use a standard calculator. It is absolutely critical to get comfortable with your financial calculator early in your preparation. Learn every function, every button, and practice extensively.

The CFA Institute is clever in its question design. If you make a common calculation error, the incorrect answer will often be one of the multiple-choice options. You might confidently select it, unaware of your mistake, because you did not thoroughly understand the calculator’s operation or the underlying concept. Avoid this trap by practicing regularly.

5. Strategic Revision and Mock Exams

Your approach to revision and mock exams can significantly impact your final score. This is where you consolidate your knowledge and build exam-day readiness.

Simultaneous Study and Revision

Do not study one subject, complete it, and then move on to the next in isolation. Instead, adopt a simultaneous study and revision strategy. For instance, if you have completed two subjects, revise them briefly before starting a third. As you add a fourth, revise all three previous subjects. This iterative process builds your mental capacity to handle multiple topics concurrently, which is essential for the breadth of the CFA exam.

Build Sitting Capacity

The CFA exam is a marathon, not a sprint. Each exam session is 2 hours and 15 minutes long, with a total testing time of 4 hours and 30 minutes. Most Indian academic exams are 3 hours. You need to build your sitting capacity to maintain focus and performance for such an extended period. Mock exams are invaluable for this. They simulate the actual exam environment, helping you build the stamina required.

Attempt Mocks ONLY After Completing Syllabus

This is a non-negotiable rule: attempt mock exams only after you have completed the entire CFA syllabus. I have seen many students make the mistake of attempting mocks after completing just a few topics. This leads to frustration and a false sense of inadequacy.

CFA mock exam questions are often inter-related, drawing concepts from various topics. For example, an Equity Investment question might incorporate principles from Economics or Quantitative Methods. If you have not studied all topics, you will struggle with these integrated questions, leading to a dip in confidence. Your confidence is a valuable asset; do not undermine it unnecessarily.

Once your syllabus is complete, take 2-3 official CFA Institute mocks and supplement them with 4-6 mocks from prep providers like Schweser. Analyze your mistakes thoroughly, understand the concepts you missed, and revisit those sections. This iterative process of mock, review, and re-study is crucial for identifying weaknesses and refining your exam strategy. For more on this, consider our advice on CFA exam time management.

6. My Personal Take: Beyond the Scores

Having guided countless individuals through this journey, here is my honest assessment and recommendation:

  • Your “Why” Must Be Clear: Vedant’s success underlines this. When the CFA is more than just an exam—when it is an emotion, a clear step towards your desired career—you will find the intrinsic motivation to put in the hours and overcome challenges.
  • The First Step is to Start: Many aspiring candidates get caught in analysis paralysis—worrying about exam dates, coaching costs, or their ability to pass. My advice is to break the inertia and start preparing. Forget the exam date initially. After two to three months of consistent preparation, you will have a much clearer, objective understanding of your progress, your pace, and when you are truly ready to schedule your exam. The CFA offers multiple exam windows (February, May, August, November), providing flexibility. Do not fall into the trap of blindly following peers; your preparation journey is unique.
  • Invest in Good Resources: While the CFA Institute curriculum is the primary source, supplemental materials like Schweser notes are excellent for condensing the vast syllabus into digestible formats. In India, you can often find these resources at a very nominal cost.
  • Stay Positive: The CFA journey is demanding. There will be moments of doubt and frustration. Maintaining a positive mindset and believing in your structured effort is paramount. If you are reading this far, it means you possess the diligence and curiosity required for success.

The CFA Program is a significant investment of time and effort, but it yields substantial returns in career growth and knowledge. Passing Level 1 is just the first hurdle. Continue to Level 2 and Level 3, and you will see a significant “hockey stick” effect in your career trajectory.
