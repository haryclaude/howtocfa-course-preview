M&A Career After CFA: Roles, Skills & Salary

Considering a mergers and acquisitions career after CFA? Learn about M&A roles, required skills, and salary expectations from an IIT-IIM alumnus.

You are considering a career in Mergers and Acquisitions—M&A. This is a profile that consistently captures the imagination of finance professionals, and for good reason. It is dynamic, intellectually challenging, and offers significant growth potential. As an IIT-IIM alumnus and CFA charterholder who has mentored hundreds of candidates, I can tell you that M&A is not just for bulge-bracket investment banks anymore. Today, even new-age startups frequently have dedicated M&A divisions, recognizing inorganic growth as a rapid path to market expansion.

Consider the recent acquisition of Blinkit by Zomato, or the numerous strategic acquisitions made by Flipkart. These are not isolated incidents; they represent a fundamental shift in how companies approach growth. The choice is often between “build versus buy”: either develop a new product or service internally, or acquire an existing business that already operates in your target market or possesses the technology you need.

Let us dissect what an M&A career truly entails and how the CFA Program can be a powerful accelerator for your ambitions in this space.

What is Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A)?

Before we dive into career specifics, it is important to understand the core concepts. While the terms “merger” and “acquisition” are often used interchangeably, there is a subtle but significant difference.

  • Merger: This occurs when two companies combine to form a new entity, and often the individual identities of the original companies are dissolved or absorbed into the new brand. The goal is typically synergy—that the combined entity is worth more than the sum of its parts. An example often cited is the proposed Sony-Zee merger, where the intent was for Zee’s brand to be absorbed by Sony’s.
  • Acquisition: This is when one company buys another company. The acquired company often continues to operate under its own brand name, but it is now owned by the acquiring company. For instance, when Zomato acquired Blinkit, Blinkit retained its brand identity. Similarly, Coca-Cola acquired Thums Up, which maintained its distinct brand and customer base.

In an M&A role, your primary objective is to identify and execute transactions that drive strategic growth for your company or your client’s company. This could involve entering new geographies, acquiring new technologies, or gaining access to new customer segments.

Consider the example of OYO. When it sought to expand into the event space, it acquired Weddings.in. When it aimed for the European vacation rental market, it acquired a local 25-year-old company, Vacation Homes, rather than building from scratch. An M&A team performs the due diligence and strategic analysis for such transactions.

How the CFA Program Prepares You for M&A

Many aspiring M&A professionals ask if the CFA Program is truly relevant. My answer is an emphatic yes. The CFA curriculum provides an “A to Z” understanding of finance, with particular emphasis on three areas crucial for M&A.

1. Financial Analysis

You must possess a deep understanding of financial statements—the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. Why? Because when you are evaluating a target company for acquisition, you cannot simply take their word for it. Companies often present their financials in the best possible light.

A strong grasp of financial analysis allows you to:

  • Identify hidden liabilities, such as deferred employee salaries that might not show up as immediate cash outflows but represent future obligations.
  • Uncover aggressive accounting practices that might inflate revenue or depress costs.
  • Understand the true operational health and financial strength of a target company.

The CFA Program trains you to look beyond the surface, to understand the nuances and sophistication of corporate financials, and to ask the right questions. You can learn more about this in our guide to CFA Level 1 Financial Reporting and Analysis.

2. Valuation

This is the cornerstone of any M&A deal. If you are buying a company, you must know what it is worth. The CFA Program dedicates significant portions of its curriculum to various valuation methodologies:

  • Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Projecting future cash flows and discounting them back to the present.
  • Multiples-based Valuation: Comparing the target company to similar publicly traded companies or recent transactions using metrics like EV/EBITDA or P/E.
  • Relative Valuation: Using comparable companies or transactions to determine a fair value.
  • Sum-of-the-Parts Valuation: Valuing individual business units separately and summing them up.

These concepts are covered extensively in the Equity Investments and Financial Analysis topics, providing you with the tools to determine a fair and justifiable price for any acquisition.

3. Ethics

Often overlooked, but critically important, is the emphasis on ethics in the CFA Program. Ethics accounts for a substantial portion of the Level I exam—typically 15-20%. This is not just theoretical.

In M&A, you gain access to highly confidential, non-public information about target companies. Knowing the CFA Institute’s Standards of Professional Conduct is paramount. It guides you on:

  • Handling material non-public information.
  • Avoiding conflicts of interest.
  • Maintaining confidentiality.
  • Ensuring fair dealing with all parties.

A breach of ethical conduct can lead to severe legal repercussions, reputational damage, and career termination. The CFA Program instills a strong ethical framework that is essential for navigating the sensitive information flows in M&A.

Alternative Paths to an M&A Career

While I advocate strongly for the CFA Program, it is certainly not the only route. Many successful M&A professionals come from diverse backgrounds. Here are some other strong credentials and pathways:

  • Premier MBA from a Top-Tier Institute: An MBA from a top 10 institute in India (e.g., the older IIMs, MDI Gurgaon, SPJIMR, NMIMS) can open doors to M&A roles, particularly at the Associate level. These programs offer strong networking opportunities and a broad business education. However, admission is highly competitive, with acceptance rates often below 1%. You can explore a comparison in our article on CFA vs MBA.
  • Chartered Accountancy (CA): The CA qualification, especially in India, provides an incredibly deep understanding of financial accounting, auditing, and taxation. This knowledge is invaluable for due diligence and understanding a company’s financial health during an M&A transaction. CA professionals are often well-regarded for their analytical rigor.
  • Certified Public Accountant (CPA): Similar to CA, the CPA is a globally recognized accounting qualification that provides extensive knowledge of financial reporting and analysis. It is highly valued, particularly in international M&A deals.
  • Specialized Certifications:
    • Certified Valuation Analyst (CVA): This certification focuses specifically on business valuation, providing in-depth knowledge of various valuation methodologies.
    • Certified M&A Advisor (CM&AA): As the name suggests, this is a niche certification specifically designed for M&A professionals, covering deal structuring, negotiation, and integration.

While these alternatives can be effective, they often come with their own challenges, be it extreme competitiveness for top MBA/CA programs or a more specialized focus that might limit career flexibility compared to the CFA.

M&A Career Progression and Essential Skills

An M&A career is a structured journey with clear progression and demanding skill requirements.

Career Progression

You typically begin your M&A journey in one of two roles:

  • Analyst: This is common for individuals with an undergraduate degree (e.g., B.Com, B.E./B.Tech). Your work involves extensive data gathering, financial modeling, presentation preparation, and market research.
  • Associate: If you hold a postgraduate degree like an MBA or a CFA charter, you might start as an Associate. This role involves more project management, client interaction, and managing analyst teams.

From there, the typical progression is:

  1. Analyst
  2. Associate
  3. Vice President (VP): You start leading smaller deals, managing client relationships, and mentoring junior team members.
  4. Director: You are responsible for sourcing deals, leading deal teams, and managing key client accounts.
  5. Managing Director (MD): This is a senior leadership role involving strategic oversight, client acquisition, and overall responsibility for the M&A practice.

Key Skills for Success in M&A

Beyond formal certifications, specific skills are critical for thriving in M&A.

1. Financial Modeling

This is non-negotiable. You must be proficient in building complex financial models in Excel to project financial performance, assess different deal structures, and perform sensitivity analysis. A strong financial model forms the backbone of any valuation and deal rationale. This skill is critical for virtually all essential skills for financial jobs.

2. Negotiation

As you progress to VP and Director levels, your negotiation skills become paramount. You are not just presenting a valuation; you are advising clients on how to close a deal at the most favorable terms. This involves understanding the counterparty’s motivations, anticipating their moves, and crafting persuasive arguments. Think about how Facebook acquired Instagram—the negotiation was not just about price, but about strategic value and future potential.

3. Strategic Thinking

M&A is inherently strategic. You must be able to think beyond immediate numbers and understand the broader market, competitive landscape, and long-term implications of a deal. My advice to develop this skill is simple: always ask “Why?”

When you encounter any piece of information—a market trend, a company announcement, a political development—do not just accept it. Ask why it is happening. Why is this company acquiring that one? Why is a particular industry consolidating? This habit trains your mind to dig deeper, connect dots, and develop a more nuanced understanding, which is vital for effective strategic decision-making.

4. Due Diligence

This is about meticulous attention to detail. Due diligence is the process of thoroughly investigating a target company to confirm its financial, legal, operational, and commercial viability. It is about “छोटी मगर मोटी बातें”—small details that can have large consequences.

For example, if a financial statement shows low costs, a diligent M&A professional would not just accept it. They would investigate whether employee salaries are being deferred, if there are pending litigations, or if key contracts are about to expire. Missing such details can lead to significant post-acquisition liabilities. Due diligence requires a skeptical mindset and the willingness to go beyond surface-level information.

Steps to Build an M&A Career

If M&A is your chosen path, here are the steps you should take:

1. Build a Strong Educational and Brand Foundation

Whether it is the CFA charter, a top-tier MBA, or a CA qualification, having a recognized brand on your resume is crucial. Human psychology often trusts brands. Just as voters often align with established political parties, employers and senior executives in M&A roles often place more confidence in candidates with reputable certifications or degrees. This provides you with credibility and gives you a chance to prove your capabilities.

2. Cultivate Networking Skills

M&A is a relationship-driven business. You cannot rely solely on financial reports and public disclosures. Often, critical insights about a target company—such as a founder’s desire to exit, internal conflicts, or unstated strategic objectives—come from informal conversations and networking.

You must be comfortable engaging with founders, CEOs, CFOs, and other senior executives, often in social settings. If you are averse to networking, M&A may not be the right fit for you. There are many other excellent finance careers that do not require this intense level of external engagement. However, if you are willing to cultivate these skills, they will serve you immensely.

My Personal Take: Why the CFA Program Stands Out

Many paths lead to an M&A career, but I believe the CFA Program offers a unique and compelling value proposition, particularly in the Indian context.

While premier MBAs and the CA qualification are undoubtedly powerful brands, they are incredibly difficult to acquire. The admission probability for a top-tier MBA in India is often less than 1%, and the pass rate for CA is typically 3-5%. These are highly competitive bottlenecks.

Specialized certifications like CVA or CM&AA are excellent for their specific domains, and they do have decent pass rates. However, their highly specialized nature presents a significant challenge: lack of flexibility. If you pursue a certification solely focused on M&A, and the M&A market experiences a downturn (as it often does during economic slowdowns, when companies conserve cash instead of pursuing growth), your career options can become limited.

The CFA Program, with its approximately 40% pass rate across levels, strikes a superior balance. It is rigorous, but attainable with structured effort and consistency. More importantly, it is a generalist finance qualification. While it provides the depth required for M&A, it also covers a vast array of topics—investment banking, hedge fund management, equity analysis, portfolio management, risk analysis, wealth management, and more. This breadth gives you immense flexibility to pivot your career if market conditions change or your interests evolve.

The CFA charter is a globally recognized mark of analytical rigor and ethical conduct. It signals to employers that you possess a comprehensive understanding of investment principles and a commitment to professional standards. For those looking to build a robust, long-term career in finance, with M&A as a potential specialization, the CFA Program is an exceptionally strong foundation. You can read more about the diverse CFA career opportunities it opens up.

If you are serious about an M&A career after CFA, focus on mastering the curriculum, building your financial modeling prowess, developing strategic thinking habits, and cultivating your networking abilities. Success in M&A, like in all demanding finance careers, is a result of structured effort, consistent discipline, and a long-term perspective.
