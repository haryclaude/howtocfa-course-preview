Cracking CFA for Average Students: My Honest Take

Can average students crack the CFA exam? Harmeet Hora, IIT-IIM & CFA, explains how consistent effort, not innate brilliance, leads to success.

One of the most frequent questions I encounter from aspiring finance professionals is often framed with a touch of self-doubt: “I am an average student. Can I truly crack the CFA exam?” As someone who has navigated the finance world from IIT to IIM and earned the coveted CFA charter, I can tell you unequivocally that the answer is a resounding yes.

Your academic past, your percentile in competitive exams, or even your perceived “average” status do not define your potential for CFA success. What truly matters is your approach, your consistency, and your dedication. Let me explain why.

Defining “Average” — It Is a Mindset Shift

Many people label themselves “average” based on past academic performance—perhaps a B.Com degree from a Tier 3 college, or struggles with mathematics in school. Here is my honest assessment: an “average” student is someone who remains in a state of ignorance, unwilling to seek knowledge and awareness.

The moment you decide to question your limitations, to seek guidance, and to understand how you can improve, you cease to be “average.” You become a proactive learner, someone committed to growth. This shift in mindset is the first, and perhaps most crucial, step towards conquering the CFA Program. I congratulate you on taking that step simply by reading this.

Academic Background Does Not Dictate Success

Forget the notion that you need to be an IIT or IIM alumnus, or possess a 90+ percentile in CAT, to clear the CFA exam. That is simply not true. I have personally mentored hundreds of students who, by conventional societal standards, were considered “average”—some even had marks as low as 40% in high school mathematics. Yet, they not only cleared the CFA exam but also went on to build successful careers in finance.

The CFA Institute designs its curriculum to be accessible to a wide range of individuals. It is not about innate brilliance; it is about structured effort, disciplined study, and a practical approach to learning. Whether you are a B.Com graduate, an engineer, or come from any other background, your past grades are not an impediment. Your commitment moving forward is what counts.

The CFA Program Demands a Clear “Why”

Before embarking on any significant professional journey, especially one as demanding as the CFA Program, you must establish your “why.” What is your ultimate career goal? I recently encountered a query from a candidate interested in both finance and HR, confused about how CFA would help with both.

These are fundamentally different domains. If your passion lies in Human Resources, then an MBA with an HR specialization from institutions like SIBM Pune or XLRI Jamshedpur would be the appropriate path. The CFA charter is designed for careers in investment management and finance.

If your “why” is rooted in building a career in finance—be it in investment banking, asset management, equity research, or portfolio management—then the CFA Program is an excellent choice. This clarity provides the internal motivation necessary to sustain you through the rigorous preparation. Without a clear “why,” even the brightest minds can falter.

You can explore various CFA career paths in India to align your “why” with potential roles.

Consistent Effort Over Innate Brilliance

The CFA Program is a marathon, not a sprint. Success is built on consistency, not on cramming or pulling all-nighters. Many candidates believe they need to devote 10-12 hours a day to study. This is simply not true.

Based on my experience mentoring thousands of students, a commitment of 90 minutes of focused study every single day is often sufficient to prepare for the CFA Level I exam within six to nine months.

Consider the diverse study timelines:

  • US Students: Many of my students from the US, who balance social life, sports, and other commitments, often spend 30-45 minutes daily and clear the exam in 12 months.
  • CA Students: Due to significant overlap in curriculum, many CA students can prepare in as little as three months.
  • Indian Working Professionals: A typical six to nine-month window is common for those balancing work and study.

The key takeaway is that even with constraints from work, graduation, or other preparations, you do not need to sacrifice everything. Consistent, dedicated effort for a manageable period each day will yield results.

Strategy for Success: Start Early, Practice Smart

One common mistake I observe is candidates booking their exam date first, then commencing preparation. This is often a herd mentality, driven by what peers are doing. I strongly advise against this.

1. Begin Preparation First

Instead, start your preparation first. Dive into the curriculum without the pressure of an immediate exam date. After two to three months of consistent study, you will gain three critical insights:

  • Understanding the World of Finance: The CFA curriculum will unfold the vast and complex world of finance. You will understand if this domain truly excites you.
  • Gauging Your Pace: You will objectively assess your learning momentum and estimate how long it will take you to complete the course comprehensively.
  • Confirming Your Interest: In a small percentage of cases, you might discover that finance, despite initial interest, does not resonate with you. This is a valuable realization. Spending a few thousand rupees on coaching to discover this is far better than investing lakhs in exam fees and registration, only to drop out later.

The CFA exam is offered four times a year (February, May, August, November). This flexibility means you are not bound by a single date. You can align your exam attempt with your genuine readiness.

2. Focus on Practical Application, Not Rote Learning

The CFA exam is not about memorizing formulas or definitions. It is about understanding concepts and applying them logically. I emphasize a practical approach in my lectures, using real-world examples from the financial and business domains. This helps concepts “sink into your heart,” not just your mind.

For instance, when we discuss fiscal policy, I relate it to real-life budget speeches. Monetary policy discussions connect directly to RBI announcements. This makes complex topics relatable and easier to grasp. We also integrate Excel applications where relevant, ensuring you are prepared for the practical demands of a finance role, not just the exam.

Tackling “Weak” Subjects: Quant and Economics

Many “average” students express concern about quantitative methods (Quant) and economics. Let us address these head-on.

Quantitative Methods

Even with my engineering background from IIT Kharagpur, I found some aspects of Quant challenging. This is because it introduces new, advanced statistical and analytical concepts. However, the key is to approach it logically and with practical examples.

Consider hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, or normal distribution. These can seem theoretical and hard in isolation. But think about exit polls during election season. When pollsters predict a political party will win 300-350 seats, that range is essentially a confidence interval in action. Understanding this real-world application makes the concept far more accessible.

The CFA exam does not demand heavy, complex mathematical calculations. Instead, it tests your logical understanding of these concepts—their advantages, disadvantages, and appropriate use cases. If your concepts are strong, you will navigate the multiple-choice questions effectively.

Practice is paramount for Quant. Utilize the CFA Institute curriculum, Schweser notes, and mock exams extensively. Repetition solidifies your understanding.

Economics

This might surprise you, but in my six years of mentoring thousands of students, I have found economics to be one of the easiest topics in the CFA Level I curriculum. If you find it difficult, it often indicates an incorrect approach.

Economics is inherently connected to our daily lives. When you read about fiscal deficit, monetary policy, or interest rates, these are not abstract theories; they are concepts constantly discussed in financial news and policy debates. When Amazon acquires a company, it is a real-world example of market dynamics and corporate strategy.

If you are struggling, it is likely because you are studying these concepts in isolation. You must correlate them with real-life examples. Start reading a financial newspaper regularly. Observe how the Reserve Bank of India discusses monetary policy or how the Finance Ministry comments on fiscal policies. Editorial pieces and articles provide practical context that will make these concepts intuitive.

The CFA Program requires a well-rounded understanding across all 10 topics. You cannot afford to neglect any section, aiming to compensate weak areas with strong ones. The goal is to achieve 50-60% or more in every section.

The CFA Exam Structure and Flexibility

The CFA Program is a globally recognized and respected credential, with a brand value built over 60 years. It provides a comprehensive understanding of finance, covering topics from ethics and quantitative methods to equity investments and derivatives.

The multiple-choice format of the exam, combined with its logical questioning style, allows you to demonstrate conceptual mastery. The fact that the Level I exam is offered four times a year provides immense flexibility. This is why I advocate for starting preparation first and then deciding on your exam date. This approach removes undue pressure and allows for optimal timing based on your readiness. You can learn more about CFA eligibility and registration here.

My Personal Take

Let me be direct: Your past academic record, whether you consider it “average” or not, is not a barrier to cracking the CFA exam. What will define your success is your commitment to consistency, your willingness to learn practically, and your disciplined effort.

The CFA Program is a testament to the power of structured learning and perseverance. It is not about innate brilliance; it is about the grit to show up every day, understand the concepts, and practice diligently. I have seen countless students, just like you, transform their careers through this journey. You absolutely can do it.
