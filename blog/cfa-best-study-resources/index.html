Navigating the CFA Program demands more than just intelligence; it requires a strategic approach to your study materials. As an IIT-IIM alumnus and CFA charterholder, I have mentored hundreds of candidates through this rigorous journey. A common question I receive revolves around selecting the best CFA study resources and materials. The answer is not always straightforward, but it is certainly achievable with a clear strategy.

Today, I want to share insights derived from the experiences of many successful candidates, including one of my mentees, Anjali. Her journey highlights the critical role of choosing the right resources and, perhaps more importantly, cultivating the right mindset.

The Overlooked Factor: Mental Resilience in CFA Preparation

Many candidates focus solely on the academic challenge of the CFA Program. However, the mental game is equally, if not more, critical. Anjali’s first attempt at CFA Level I serves as a powerful reminder of this.

She carried a heavy burden of past failures and immense stress, exacerbated by external factors like the COVID-19 pandemic. This mental baggage, coupled with personal health issues, severely hampered her focus and performance. It is a common trap: allowing anxiety about potential failure or external pressures to overshadow the actual learning process.

Here is my honest assessment: You cannot perform at your best if your mental state is compromised. The CFA exam is not merely a test of your financial knowledge; it is a test of your endurance, your discipline, and your ability to manage stress. Many candidates experience similar pressures; you are not alone. For more on navigating exam stress, consider reading our guide on /blog/cfa-exam-overcoming-failure/.

From Setback to Strategy: Anjali’s Pivot

Anjali’s story does not end with a setback; it begins a journey of remarkable resilience. After her initial failure, she chose to take a complete break for an entire year. This was a brave decision, especially in a culture where breaks are often stigmatized. She disconnected from academics, pursued hobbies like painting, crochet, and gardening, and spent quality time with her family.

This period was not a pause in her journey but a crucial reset. It allowed her to address burnout, regain her mental strength, and approach the exam with a fresh perspective. Her family’s support was instrumental in helping her overcome the embarrassment of failure and realize that it was “just another exam,” not a defining moment of her life.

When she felt ready, she cautiously re-engaged. She started by ordering Kaplan Schweser books and spent four to six weeks studying the curriculum on her own, without the pressure of an immediate exam date. This self-paced re-introduction allowed her to enjoy the learning process again, building confidence before committing to registration.

Core CFA Study Resources: A Head-to-Head Comparison

When discussing CFA study materials, two primary categories dominate the conversation:

  1. CFA Institute Curriculum: The official study material provided by the CFA Institute.
  2. Third-Party Providers: Commercial prep providers like Kaplan Schweser, Mark Meldrum, and others.

Anjali’s experience highlights the strengths and weaknesses of each. In her first attempt, she relied solely on Kaplan Schweser and completely skipped the official curriculum. This was a critical error. In her second attempt, she made sure to “devour” the Institute material.

1. CFA Institute Curriculum: The Holy Grail

Anjali realized that the CFA Institute provides its curriculum for a reason. It is not merely supplementary; it is the foundation.

  • Depth and Detail: The Institute curriculum provides the most comprehensive and authoritative explanation of all learning outcomes. Its explanations are exceptionally well-curated and detailed.
  • Examples and Context: It offers numerous real-world examples that help cement theoretical concepts, often linking them to practical financial applications. These examples are invaluable for understanding the nuances required for the exam.
  • End-of-Chapter Questions (EOCs): The EOC questions are arguably the most important practice questions available. They align perfectly with the exam style and difficulty, directly testing your understanding of the learning outcome statements (LOS).
  • Accuracy: It is the official source. Any ambiguities or disputes about content are ultimately resolved by referring to the Institute’s materials.

The downside: It is lengthy and can feel overwhelming. Many candidates find it dense and time-consuming to read cover-to-cover.

2. Third-Party Providers (e.g., Kaplan Schweser)

Providers like Kaplan Schweser have become incredibly popular due to their ability to condense the vast curriculum.

  • Conciseness: These materials distill the core concepts, making them easier to digest and review. They cut through the “extra knowledge” that might not be directly tested.
  • Structured Learning: They often provide well-organized notes, summaries, and lecture videos that can accelerate initial understanding.
  • Practice Questions: Third-party providers offer a wealth of practice questions, mock exams, and question banks, which are essential for reinforcing learning and identifying weak areas.

The downside: While concise, they may lack the depth and nuanced explanations found in the official curriculum. Relying solely on them can leave gaps in your understanding, especially for complex topics where the exam may test subtleties.

My Prescriptive Approach: Integrating Your Study Materials

Having seen numerous candidates succeed and struggle, my recommendation is to adopt a blended strategy. You cannot afford to skip the CFA Institute Curriculum entirely, but you also do not need to read every single word.

Here is how I recommend you integrate these resources:

  1. Start with Third-Party Notes (Initial Pass): Begin your study of each reading with a third-party provider’s notes or video lectures. This provides a high-level overview and helps you grasp the core concepts quickly. Think of it as mapping out the terrain.
  2. Deep Dive with CFA Institute Curriculum (Targeted Learning): Once you have a foundational understanding from the third-party materials, switch to the CFA Institute Curriculum. Focus on:
    • Learning Outcome Statements (LOS): Ensure you can address each LOS comprehensively.
    • Key Concepts and Formulas: Understand the derivation and application of crucial formulas.
    • Examples: Pay close attention to the examples provided. They often illustrate the exact scenarios tested on the exam.
    • End-of-Chapter Questions (EOCs): Complete ALL EOC questions. These are non-negotiable. Review the explanations for incorrect answers thoroughly.
  3. Supplement with Third-Party Question Banks: After going through the Institute curriculum and EOCs, use third-party question banks for additional practice. This helps you reinforce concepts and identify any remaining weak areas.
  4. Review and Reinforce: Use the concise third-party notes for quick revisions, especially in the weeks leading up to the exam.

This approach allows you to leverage the efficiency of third-party materials for initial learning while ensuring you develop the deep understanding and analytical skills that only the official curriculum can provide. Beyond the Books: The Power of Practice and Application

Your preparation does not end with reading. The true test of understanding comes with application.

  1. Practice Questions: Beyond EOCs, engage with a wide variety of practice questions from different sources. This exposes you to various question styles and helps solidify your knowledge.
  2. Mock Exams: Schedule multiple mock exams in the final 6-8 weeks leading up to your exam date. Treat these as real exams—timed, under strict conditions. This is crucial for:
    • Time Management: Learning to allocate time effectively across different sections and questions.
    • Stamina: Building the mental and physical endurance for a six-hour exam.
    • Identifying Weaknesses: Pinpointing areas where your understanding is still shaky.
    • Strategy: Developing a test-taking strategy that works for you.
  3. Revision: Dedicate ample time for revision. This means reviewing notes, re-doing difficult questions, and consolidating your understanding. Spaced repetition can be very effective here.
  4. Real-World Context: Whenever possible, try to link the concepts you are learning to real-world financial news or business scenarios. This makes the material more engaging and helps you internalize the principles. For example, understanding fiscal and monetary policy is much easier when you connect it to current economic headlines. This practical application is a cornerstone of success, particularly for those aiming for roles in investment banking after CFA.

My Personal Take: Discipline, Not Just Dollars, Drives Success

The CFA Program is an investment—of time, effort, and money. Evaluating your study resources through a cost-benefit lens is logical. However, the greatest return on investment comes not from spending on the most expensive materials, but from the discipline and consistency you apply to your studies.

Anjali’s journey underscores this perfectly. Her initial failure was not due to a lack of resources, but a lack of mental fortitude and an incomplete strategy. Her success came from a renewed mindset, a structured approach to leveraging both official and third-party materials, and unwavering persistence.

You do not need innate brilliance to pass the CFA exam. What you need is structured effort, consistent study habits, and the resilience to learn from setbacks. This journey is about long-term career thinking, building a robust foundation in finance, rather than chasing quick wins.
