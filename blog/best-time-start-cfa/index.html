Best Time to Start CFA Preparation: My Honest Take

Unsure when to start your CFA journey? Harmeet Hora, IIT-IIM & CFA, shares his honest assessment on the best time to start CFA preparation.

The question of “When is the best time to start CFA preparation?” is one I hear frequently from aspiring finance professionals. As an IIT-IIM alumnus and CFA charterholder who has navigated this journey and mentored hundreds of candidates, I can tell you that while there is no single “perfect” answer for everyone, there is certainly an optimal approach for most.

Let us dissect this question with the practicality and long-term career thinking that defines success in finance. We will consider eligibility, career goals, and the strategic advantages of timing.

Define Your “Why”: The Crucial First Step

Before you even consider when to start, you must answer why you want to pursue the CFA Program. This is not a trivial question. The CFA charter is a significant commitment of time, effort, and financial resources. Without a clear “why,” your motivation will waver, and the probability of success diminishes significantly.

Do not join the herd simply because your peers are doing it, or because someone told you it is a good qualification. That is a recipe for wasted effort.

Your Personal Motivation Matters

Think about what truly draws you to finance.

  • Are you fascinated by the stock market and eager to understand how companies are valued?
  • Do you aspire to work in investment banking, mergers and acquisitions, or private equity?
  • Perhaps you envision a career in portfolio management, managing wealth for individuals or institutions.
  • Are you an engineer or a science student looking to transition into the analytical rigor of finance? We have discussed this path in detail here: CFA for Science and Engineering Students.

My own journey, after IIT and IIM, led me to pursue the CFA charter because I had a very clear objective: I wanted to build a career in front-end finance roles with exponential growth potential. This clarity fueled my dedication to clear all three levels by 2012. Many of my students share similar aspirations, whether it is to become a mutual fund analyst or a hedge fund manager.

Your “why” will be unique. Take the time to identify it. This introspection is critical; it is the foundation upon which your entire CFA journey will rest. If you are unsure about potential career paths, I recommend exploring our comprehensive guide on CFA career opportunities.

CFA Eligibility: Understanding the Rules

Once your “why” is firmly established, the next practical consideration is eligibility. The CFA Institute has specific requirements for registering for Level I. You must meet one of the following criteria:

  1. A bachelor’s degree or equivalent: You must have completed a bachelor’s degree program or be in the final year of your bachelor’s degree program at the time of registration.
  2. Professional work experience: You must have at least 4,000 hours of relevant work experience, accumulated over a minimum of three years. This experience does not need to be investment-related.
  3. A combination of education and work experience: A combination of collegiate and professional work experience that totals at least 4,000 hours and has been acquired over a minimum of three years.

The most relevant point for many students, especially those in undergraduate programs like B.Tech or B.Com, is the ability to register in the final year of their bachelor’s degree. Specifically, you can register for the Level I exam if your graduation date—the date your degree is awarded—is 24 months or less from the month of the Level I exam you are registering for.

For instance, if you are in your fifth semester of a four-year B.Tech program, you are likely within this 24-month window for a future Level I exam. This means you do not need to wait until you hold your degree certificate to begin your CFA journey.

The Strategic Advantage of an Early Start

If your “why” is clear and you meet the eligibility criteria, my honest assessment is this: start as early as possible. There is a profound strategic advantage to beginning your CFA preparation during your undergraduate studies or early in your career.

1. Less Pressure, Better Absorption

During your undergraduate years, particularly in the earlier semesters, you typically have more flexibility in your schedule compared to when you enter the workforce. Juggling a demanding full-time job with CFA studies—a reality for many working professionals—can be extremely challenging. We have addressed this in our post on CFA for working professionals.

An early start allows you to:

  • Pace yourself: You can dedicate a consistent, manageable number of hours each week without feeling overwhelmed. This promotes deeper understanding and retention, rather than rote memorization under pressure.
  • Build a strong foundation: The CFA curriculum is cumulative. A solid grasp of Level I concepts makes Level II and Level III significantly easier. Starting early gives you the luxury of truly mastering these foundational topics.

2. Enhanced Resume and Internship Opportunities

In the competitive Indian finance landscape, a “CFA Level I Candidate” designation on your resume, especially while still in college, signals serious intent and analytical capability. It tells potential employers that you are proactive, disciplined, and genuinely committed to a career in finance.

This can be a significant differentiator when applying for internships or entry-level roles. Many top finance firms in India value CFA candidates, as it indicates a strong theoretical base and a commitment to professional development. It can open doors to opportunities that might otherwise be out of reach.

3. Time to Build Practical Skills

The CFA Program provides robust theoretical knowledge. However, the real world of finance also demands practical skills, particularly proficiency in tools like Microsoft Excel. When you study at a more relaxed pace, you have the bandwidth to simultaneously develop these essential skills.

I always emphasize linking theoretical concepts to their real-world application. For example, understanding discounted cash flow (DCF) models from the curriculum is one thing; building one from scratch in Excel is another. An early start provides the time to bridge this gap.

4. Networking and Mentorship

Starting early also allows you more time to engage with the CFA community and seek mentorship. You can attend local CFA Institute events, connect with charterholders, and gain insights into various career paths. This proactive networking can be invaluable for career guidance and future job prospects.

The Myth of Waiting Until “After Engineering”

Many engineering students, like the one who posed the question, contemplate waiting until they complete their B.Tech degree before starting the CFA Program. My advice is clear: do not wait.

If your “why” is strong and your path to finance is clear, delaying your CFA Level I preparation only postpones your entry into the specialized finance workforce. Your engineering degree provides an excellent analytical foundation, but the CFA complements it directly with finance-specific knowledge.

  • Opportunity Cost: Every month you delay is a month you could have been progressing towards your charter. In a fast-moving industry like finance, early momentum is critical.
  • Complementary Skills: Engineering teaches problem-solving, logical thinking, and quantitative skills—all highly valuable in finance. The CFA curriculum then layers financial modeling, valuation, economics, and portfolio management on top of this strong base. This synergy is powerful. You can read more about how these skills translate in our post on Engineering to CFA.
  • Competitive Edge: By the time you graduate with both your engineering degree and potentially CFA Level I or even Level II under your belt, you will possess a distinct competitive advantage over peers who are only just beginning their finance journey.

How to Structure Your Preparation for Success

An early start does not mean a casual approach. The CFA Program demands structured effort, consistency, and discipline.

1. Allocate Sufficient Time

The CFA Institute recommends approximately 300 hours of study for each level. This is a minimum benchmark. For Level I, I typically advise a dedicated six-month preparation window:

  • Months 1-4: Curriculum Coverage. Focus on understanding each topic area thoroughly. Do not rush. Make sure you grasp the underlying concepts.
  • Months 5-6: Revision and Practice. This phase is critical. It should involve extensive practice questions, review of weaker areas, and at least 3-6 full-length mock exams.

Consistency is more important than sporadic bursts of intense study. Aim for daily study sessions, even if they are shorter.

2. Choose Your Study Resources Wisely

There are numerous resources available for CFA preparation, including the official CFA Institute curriculum, third-party providers, and online lecture series. I recommend a multi-faceted approach:

  • CFA Institute Curriculum: This is the authoritative source. Use it for core readings and end-of-chapter questions.
  • Third-Party Providers: These often simplify complex topics and provide condensed notes and extensive question banks.
  • Video Lectures: For visual learners, video lectures can be invaluable for clarifying difficult concepts. My own lecture series, for instance, focuses on breaking down complex financial concepts with real-world examples and practical applications, including how to use Excel.

For a detailed breakdown of resources, refer to our guide on CFA best study resources.

3. Integrate Practical Application

The CFA Program is designed to be highly relevant to the finance industry. As you study, constantly think about how the concepts apply in real business scenarios.

  • Excel Practice: Practice building simple financial models, performing ratio analysis, and creating charts in Excel. This enhances your practical proficiency.
  • News and Market Awareness: Read financial news daily. Try to connect what you are learning in the curriculum to current market events and economic policies.

This holistic approach ensures that your knowledge is not just theoretical but also applicable, which is what employers truly seek.

My Personal Take and Recommendation

Here is my honest assessment: The best time to start your CFA preparation is as soon as you meet the eligibility criteria and have a clear, well-defined “why” for pursuing a career in finance.

For an engineering student in their fifth semester, this means you are likely already in the optimal window to register for Level I. Do not delay. Leverage your strong analytical foundation from engineering, combine it with a structured approach to the CFA curriculum, and you will position yourself for a highly rewarding career in finance.

Success in the CFA Program, and in finance generally, comes from structured effort, consistency, and discipline. It is not about innate brilliance; it is about strategic planning and relentless execution. Start early, stay consistent, and maintain your clarity of purpose.
